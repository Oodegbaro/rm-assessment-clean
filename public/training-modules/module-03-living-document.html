Module 3 · Keep the Plan Alive Between Reviews

Step 1 of 6 · Hook
HOOK

Your colleague inherits your account tomorrow. Could they run the meeting from the plan alone?

Efua is asked to cover a colleague's portfolio for two weeks while he's on leave. Before her first client call, she opens his account plan to get up to speed.

The plan looks tidy — stage, probability, next step, all filled in. One problem: it hasn't been touched since the last quarterly review, four months ago. Since then, the client turned down the exact facility that's still marked “70% — closing this quarter.” Efua almost repeats the same pitch in her first call, because nothing in the plan told her it had already failed.

This is the single most common way a sound account plan quietly stops being useful: not a bad plan, an old one.

THE BEHAVIOUR

After every client interaction, update at least one field — stage, probability, or next step — within about 48 hours. Don't wait for someone to ask for it.

That's the whole module. Everything from here on is how to do exactly that, using Efua's own meetings as the walk-through.

Why this matters, in one line: the plan goes stale exactly when it matters most — right before a decision gets made on it, not during some quiet week when nobody's looking at it.

This is the habit that keeps account planning honest between formal reviews. It isn't a rewrite. It's usually a two-minute edit.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Solera Foods Ghana Ltd

The meeting, in brief. Efua has just left a 40-minute check-in with Linda Owusu-Ansah, Solera's Ghana Finance Manager. Two things came up that weren't in the plan before today: Abena Sarfo, the newly appointed Ghana Managing Director, is visiting distributors next month — a possible opening for the trade finance conversation Efua has been building. And Group Treasury, run by Kwame Boateng, is reviewing FX policy at the parent level next quarter, which could open or close the FX conversation Access wants to have.

Efua's phone already has three other things on it before lunch. It would be easy to think “good meeting, I'll write it up properly later” and move on.

The standing rule. Every interaction updates at least one field — before Efua moves to the next task, not “once I'm properly back at my desk.” It doesn't need to be a full rewrite. It needs to be true and current, today.

“Later” is where updates go to die. There's always something more urgent right after a client meeting ends — and the plan feels fine, right up until someone needs to act on it and finds it's out of date.

What Efua updates right now (about two minutes):

Next step: contact Abena Sarfo's office ahead of the distributor visit
UPDATE NOW
Pipeline stage: trade finance moves from Identified to Engaging
UPDATE NOW
Probability: nudged up — the MD visit is a real door, not a rumour
UPDATE NOW
Relationship map: flag that Kwame Boateng owns FX policy timing
UPDATE NOW

What can wait for the quarterly refresh:

Company overview — ownership structure, sector detail
CAN WAIT
Full financial performance write-up
CAN WAIT
Reformatting the plan for the review deck
CAN WAIT

Minimum viable update and full rewrite are different tasks with different time costs. Doing the two-minute version today doesn't excuse Efua from the fuller refresh later — it just means the plan stays true in between.

The rhythm this feeds. This small habit, repeated after every interaction, is what makes two other rhythms actually work. Weekly, Efua scans her whole pipeline for anything stalled or overdue — a five-minute look, not a rewrite. Quarterly, a full plan refresh happens with fresh eyes, building on real updates, not starting from a blank page.

None of that works if the daily habit doesn't happen. A weekly or quarterly review can only be as current as the updates that fed it.

HOOK

One AI-drafted number nobody checked — until the client did.

Efua asked an AI tool to draft a first-pass call memo for the next Solera review. It came back fast, well organised, and confident. She tidied a sentence and sent it up the chain.

In the room, a colleague glanced at one figure and asked, "Where's this number from?" It wasn't from anywhere real. The AI had produced a confident-sounding figure that matched nothing in Efua's own notes — and no one had checked it before it reached the table.

The draft wasn't the problem. Sending it unchecked was.

THE BEHAVIOUR

Let the AI draft the first pass. You verify every figure and every claim before it goes anywhere near a client.

That's the whole module. Everything from here on is how Efua does exactly that — using a structured prompt to get a better first draft, and a short verification habit to make sure it's safe to send.

Why this matters, in one line: an AI tool can write confidently and be wrong at the same time. Confidence is not the same thing as accuracy.

You'll meet one framework by name here — SCOPE — because this module is specifically about how to prompt and verify well. It won't reappear elsewhere in this series.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Drafting Solera's call memo

The task. Efua needs a short call memo ahead of the next Solera review — something the account team can read in two minutes before the meeting. Instead of starting from a blank page, she opens Access's AI drafting tool. But she doesn't type a vague request. She builds a structured prompt first.

The SCOPE shape. She works through five things the AI needs before it can draft anything useful:

  • S — Source pack: she pastes in only what's real — her last call notes and the wallet-map fact base. Nothing pulled from the internet.
  • C — Client archetype: a mid-sized FMCG subsidiary, transactional relationship only, her contact is a gatekeeper, not a decision-maker.
  • O — Output format: a one-page memo — purpose, discussion points, figures with source, next steps.
  • P — Proof-and-gaps: tag every figure with where it came from; mark anything unsourced as a gap, not a guess.
  • E — Escalate-to-action: end with a confidence rating and a plain list of what still needs a human check.

The draft comes back. It reads well. One line says: "Solera Foods Ghana Ltd recorded FY25 revenue of approximately GHS 2.4bn, a strong increase on the prior year." It sounds entirely plausible.

But Efua's own wallet-map notes — built from Solera's FY25 update — say GHS 2.1bn. Two figures, both stated as fact, in the same account file.

Checking, not trusting. Efua looks back at her source pack. GHS 2.1bn is in there, sourced. GHS 2.4bn appears nowhere she gave the tool — which makes it invented, not just a different estimate. She corrects the memo to GHS 2.1bn, marks it "confirmed per FY25 update," and adds one open item to the gap list: reconfirm the current run rate with Linda before the review, since even the sourced figure is a few months old.

What actually leaves her desk. One figure, one source, one flagged follow-up — not two competing numbers and a guess. The AI drafted it. Efua decided whether it was accurate enough to send.

Zero invented figures reach a client. If it isn't in the source pack, it's a gap — not something for the AI to fill in.

JOB AID

AI drafting & verification — keep this

Use this every time you ask an AI tool to draft account material — once before you write the prompt, and again before anything leaves your desk.

The SCOPE prompt shape

  • S — Source pack: only real material — call notes, CRM, wallet map. Never "look this up for me."
  • C — Client archetype: sector, relationship stage, decision-maker vs gatekeeper.
  • O — Output format: the exact structure you need, spelled out.
  • P — Proof-and-gaps: every figure tagged with its source; anything unsourced marked as a gap, not filled in.
  • E — Escalate-to-action: a confidence rating, plus a plain list of what still needs a human check.

Before this leaves your desk, have you checked:

  • Every figure against a real source?
  • Every stakeholder name and title?
  • Every claimed commitment or next step?

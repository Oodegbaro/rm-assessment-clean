Module 4 · Map Who Actually Decides

Step 1 of 6 · Hook
HOOK

A facility sat in committee for four months — and nobody knew why.

A colleague's team had been chasing a mid-sized facility for a client for months. Every conversation with the finance contact went well. Then it stalled — not with a "no", just silence. When someone finally asked, plainly, "who actually has to say yes to this?", it turned out to be someone in the client's regional office the account team had never once spoken to.

Efua's own warmest relationship at Solera is with Linda Owusu-Ansah, the Ghana Finance Manager. Linda replies within the hour and always makes time for a call. It feels like a strong relationship — partly because it's such an easy one. But Efua has never met Solera's Group CFO or Group Treasury: the people who actually hold sign-off on anything beyond day-to-day banking.

Comfort isn't the same as influence. A warm contact and the person who actually decides can be two completely different people.

THE BEHAVIOUR

Before you ask a senior sponsor for help, map who actually decides — not just who you already know.

That's the whole module: sort everyone connected to a decision into decision-maker, influencer, or gatekeeper; note how each of them feels about Access; and name whoever's missing from the picture entirely — before deciding whether the gap is big enough to bring in help.

Why this matters, in one line: the person who answers your calls fastest is not automatically the person who signs the facility.

This sits right next to wallet-share mapping: one shows what's missing, this shows who actually has to agree before you can close it.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Solera Foods Ghana Ltd, revisited

Efua already knows the wallet gaps. Lending, FX, trade finance, salary mandate — all missing. Before she pursues any of them, she needs to know who actually has to agree, starting with the four people she has some contact with today: Linda Owusu-Ansah, Kojo Antwi-Danso, Abena Sarfo, and Kwame Boateng.

Her first move isn't to guess who matters most. It's to sort each of them, plainly, by what they actually control — and how they seem to feel about Access.

Step 1 — sort each contact by category and sentiment.

Linda Owusu-Ansah — Ghana Finance Manager. Warm, always responsive — but no sign-off authority beyond routine banking.
GATEKEEPER
Positive
Kojo Antwi-Danso — Group CFO, regional office. Holds real sign-off on any facility of size — Efua has never met him.
DECISION-MAKER
Unknown
Abena Sarfo — newly appointed Ghana MD. Could champion Access locally, but she's new and still building her own standing.
INFLUENCER
Positive, cautious
Kwame Boateng — Group Treasury, parent-liaison. Owns FX and cash-pooling policy for the group — protective of existing arrangements.
INFLUENCER
Resistant

Step 2 — check the numbers, lightly. Most accounts this size have somewhere around three to five people who actually matter to a decision. Efua has four names on her list — and only one of them, Kojo, holds real sign-off. She's never met him.

Four contacts isn't a weak map. But if the one confirmed decision-maker is a stranger, the map still has a hole in the middle of it.

Step 3 — look for who's missing. At a multinational subsidiary like Solera, facilities above a certain size often also need sign-off from a procurement function or a board finance committee — general corporate-structure realism, not a specific figure. Neither appears anywhere on Efua's list. That's not a small gap: it's an entire layer of the decision she has no visibility into at all.

Step 4 — decide whether to escalate. Efua's only confirmed decision-maker is someone she's never met, and there's a layer above her existing contacts she hasn't even identified yet. That combination — real authority sitting outside every relationship she has — is exactly when it's worth asking her own relationship-level leadership at Access whether they already have a way in, rather than trying to talk her way past Linda alone.

A gap is worth escalating when the person who decides is someone you have no route to on your own.

Module 5 · Get There Before the Window Closes

Step 1 of 6 · Hook
HOOK

Efua just found out — after the fact — that a client's facility renewed somewhere else.

Two weeks ago, one of Efua's accounts renewed a major facility. She heard about it only when the Finance Manager mentioned it in passing — the paperwork was already signed.

Nothing had gone wrong in the relationship. Efua simply hadn't looked ahead. The maturity date had sat in the file for months — she'd never asked what it meant for when to start talking.

By the deadline, the client has often already framed the mandate around whoever got there first — usually a competitor. Two weeks "too late" isn't bad luck. It's the default outcome of waiting to be prompted.

This is the gap this module closes: not effort, not relationship quality — timing.

THE BEHAVIOUR

Spot the trigger on file before it becomes urgent — then book the conversation well before the deadline, not at it.

That's the whole module. Everything from here on is how Efua does exactly that, using a real account as the walk-through.

Why this matters, in one line: by the time a facility matures or a tender opens, the client has often already decided — the conversation that changes the outcome happens weeks or months earlier, not on the day.

This is what turns Efua from reactive to ahead of the client's own calendar — nothing new to research, just a habit of reading what's already sitting in the file.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Solera Foods Ghana Ltd

The account, in brief. Prepping an unrelated call, Efua notices two facts sitting in Solera's file for weeks: a capex-linked facility held with another bank, maturing in roughly 90 days, and a new Ghana Managing Director, Abena Sarfo.

Neither fact is new. Nothing required fresh research — just noticing what was already written down.

Step 1 — name the trigger type(s). Efua checks the facts against the common trigger types worth watching for.

Facility / loan maturity — other bank's facility, ~90 days out
PRESENT
Capex cycle — the facility is tied to a capex programme
LINKED
Leadership change — new MD, first months reviewing banking partners
PRESENT
Tender / budget cycle
NOT YET VISIBLE

Two triggers, not one, are converging on roughly the same window — that raises the priority, not the complexity.

Step 2 — set the lead time, not the deadline. A reasonable planning habit: start the conversation somewhere in a 60–90 day window before a known maturity — a general planning guide, not a researched figure, but a working rule instead of no rule at all.

Counting back from Solera's ~90-day maturity, Efua is near the outer edge of that window — the right time to start is this week, not next month. Large facilities are often decided well before the paperwork is signed, which is exactly why "at the deadline" outreach is usually already too late.

Step 3 — decide who else needs to know. Efua doesn't run this alone: she flags it to her credit colleague, so facility appetite is sense-checked before anything is promised, and tells her team lead early, so a fast response isn't a one-person scramble.

She also maps who to reach at Solera: Linda Owusu-Ansah is warm and accessible, but a gatekeeper, not a decision-maker. Kojo Antwi-Danso, the Group CFO, holds real decision rights and Efua has never met him. Abena Sarfo, the new MD, is worth an early courtesy introduction while she's still forming views on banking partners.

Step 4 — choose the opening move. Efua doesn't open with "is your facility up for renewal soon" — that presumes insider knowledge she doesn't have and puts Linda on the spot. Instead, she asks Linda for a short introductory conversation framed around the new MD's arrival and Solera's upcoming capex planning — something Linda can say yes to without needing to admit anything is under review.

The opening move is a reason to talk that doesn't require the client to admit anything is up for review yet.
